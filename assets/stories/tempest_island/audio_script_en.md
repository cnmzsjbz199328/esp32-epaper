# The Tempest

## Global Voice Direction

English narration, tense and clear at the opening, shaped by power struggles in the middle, and gradually turning toward forgiveness and freedom. Do not present forgiveness as forgetting. Use one consistent narrator for every independently generated segment. Keep the transitions connected: “the storm that began it,” “the other side of the same island,” and “after the banquet disappears.” Do not add music or sound effects.

## English Opening — Before Scene 000

Like Homer's *Odyssey*, this is a voyage shaped by exile, dangerous seas, and the long work of finding a way home. Yet on this island, the greatest storm is not in the sky. It is the struggle between power and freedom, revenge and forgiveness, memory and the courage to begin again.

## Scene Narration

### 000 — The Storm at Sea

A violent storm drives a fleet toward an unfamiliar shore. The people aboard believe they are about to die, but Prospero on the island knows that he has arranged this storm as a reunion. The dukedom taken from him twelve years ago has finally brought his old enemies back within reach.

### 001 — The Exiled Past

Before the storm has even quieted, Prospero tells his daughter Miranda what happened twelve years ago. He was once the Duke of Milan, but his devotion to study led him to hand power to his brother. His brother joined with outsiders, seized the city, and forced father and daughter onto a small boat. The island kept them alive, and it gave Prospero time to turn knowledge into magic capable of controlling everything around him.

### 002 — Ariel's Contract

The spirit who raised this storm is Ariel. She moves through branches and rain, carrying out every command her master gives. Prospero once freed her from a split tree, but then kept her in service for years. Ariel reminds him that being rescued does not mean losing freedom forever. Every contract should have an ending.

### 003 — Caliban and the Island

There is another servant on the island. Caliban believes the island belonged to him, while Prospero treats him as someone who must obey. Both claim to have been harmed, and both try to prove ownership through force. There is no simple good or evil here, only one question echoing through the island: who has the right to decide how another person should live?

### 004 — Miranda and Ferdinand

Among the passengers scattered by the storm, Prince Ferdinand reaches the island alone and meets Miranda. In this unfamiliar place, they test one another carefully. Their love allows two hostile families to imagine reconciliation, but it also makes Prospero afraid of losing the last thing he can control. Love is not a political treaty, yet it forces politics to acknowledge real human feeling.

### 005 — The Scattered Survivors

On the other side of the same island, Alonso, Ferdinand's father, and the sailors wake believing that the prince has drowned. Grief quickly becomes suspicion, and an old alliance begins to split apart again. Losing an heir reveals what each person truly fears. It is not the storm itself, but having nowhere to stand after power is gone.

### 006 — Another Conspiracy

Amid the suspicion, Antonio and Sebastian learn nothing humble from disaster. Instead, they plan to seize a new throne while everyone is confused. They treat their earlier betrayal as a successful lesson, without seeing that Prospero is repeating the same kind of control. The island's magic reflects everyone's desire, and it also shows how a victim can become an abuser.

### 007 — The Absurd Plan to Take the Island

On another part of the island, Caliban is making his own plan for power. He meets two drunken sailors and mistakes them for allies who can overthrow Prospero. The three of them imagine a kingdom built from liquor and empty promises. Their plan is ridiculous, but it still carries a real hunger for possession. If the oppressed learn only to oppress others, a new crown will not bring freedom.

### 008 — The Vanishing Banquet

All these threads have unfolded under Prospero's watch. He summons an illusion and draws everyone to the same banquet, forcing them to face the crimes of their past. The food vanishes, the music stops, and fear replaces celebration. Punishment can make people lower their heads, but it may not make them change. Prospero realizes for the first time that the power to make everyone suffer has not brought him any closer to justice.

### 009 — The End of Revenge

After the banquet disappears, he stands alone with his magic book and staff, imagining that he could keep his old enemies on the island forever. But revenge can only prove again and again that the wound is still there. It cannot return the years that were taken. Miranda looks at her father and asks whether he wants her to inherit a world maintained by fear. Her question begins to loosen his resolve.

### 010 — Speaking the Old Truth

So Prospero appears before them. He speaks first of the dukedom taken from him, the exile forced upon him, and the anger he has carried for years. Then he listens as Alonso and the others admit their betrayal. The truth does not make anyone instantly innocent, but it gives a name to responsibility that has been silent for too long. Without truth, forgiveness is only another demand that the wounded stay quiet.

### 011 — Choosing Forgiveness

Once the truth has been spoken, Prospero finally lays down revenge. He does not claim that the past never happened. Instead, he admits that he too used magic to turn other people into prisoners. To choose forgiveness is to stop creating the same injury, and to let everyone leave carrying memory and responsibility, rather than disguising guilt as a play that has already ended.

### 012 — Laying Down Magic

He breaks his staff, and the magic book that has served him no longer commands the sea. Ariel is free. Caliban can decide what comes next. Miranda can finally leave the island her father arranged around her. Without magic, human choices will be harder, but at last they will truly belong to the people making them.

### 013 — Leaving the Island

The ship sails out across the sea, and the island remains behind. Some will return to the city. Some must face new responsibilities. Some will stay and learn again how to live. The wind will still fill the sails, but it will no longer decide anyone's direction. Freedom is not forgetting the old storm. It is remembering it, and still choosing to sail toward the future.

## English Closing — After Scene 013

The voyage ends as an odyssey should: not with the past erased, but with the travelers changed by what they have faced. The sea carries them home, while the island keeps its questions. What makes a ruler just? What does freedom require? And can mercy become the first true step toward a new life?
