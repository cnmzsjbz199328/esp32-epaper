# A Midsummer Night's Dream

## Global Voice Direction

English narration, moving between waking reality and dreamlike uncertainty. The first half should feel pressured and confused; the second half should gradually relax. Do not reduce love to a sweet game. Use one consistent narrator for every independently generated segment. Keep the transitions connected: “the same forest,” “the same night,” and “back to Athens.” Do not add music or sound effects.

## English Opening — Before Scene 000

Like Homer's *Odyssey*, this is a story of people leaving the order they know and entering a world that tests who they are. But this journey moves through an enchanted forest, where desire, power, and mistaken identity turn one night into a trial of choice and responsibility.

## Scene Narration

### 000 — The Athenian Decree

In Athens, Hermia must marry according to her father's wishes, or face severe punishment. She loves Lysander, but she is being told to disguise obedience as happiness. So the two of them decide to leave the city and search for a place where they can make their own choices.

### 001 — Into the Forest

And so, as the moon rises, Hermia and Lysander enter the forest, with law, family, and pursuing commands behind them. The forest path promises no safety. It only carries them away from familiar order, forcing each of them to face what they truly want.

### 002 — The Fairy Queen's Quarrel

Deep in the same forest, other inhabitants are fighting their own battles. Oberon, king of the fairies, and Titania quarrel over a child. Even rulers with magic can call possessiveness care, and power justice. The forest loses its balance, and the flowers and weather seem to argue on their behalf.

### 003 — A Bewitched Flower

To gain the upper hand, Oberon orders Puck to find a magical flower. Its juice, placed on the eyes of a sleeper, will make that person love the first one they see upon waking. Puck flies into the forest with a lighthearted task, but does not carefully check whom he is helping. Magic is most dangerous when it looks easy.

### 004 — The Craftsmen's Play

While Puck moves through the trees, a group of craftsmen also arrives in the forest. They are rehearsing a play for the Duke's wedding. They want to perform a tragedy with dignity, but cannot remember their lines or even their exits. Their clumsy rehearsal is funny, and it also reminds us that everyone is playing some kind of role without always knowing who they want to become.

### 005 — Love Misplaced

Puck returns with the flower juice, but places it on the wrong eyes. When Lysander wakes, he no longer loves Hermia. Friends who once loved one another begin to chase, accuse, and argue with each other, unable to trust even their own hearts. The forest magnifies hidden jealousy, turning one mistake into harm for four people.

### 006 — Bottom Transformed

That same night, Puck mistakes another man for his target and gives the craftsman Bottom the head of a donkey. Under the spell, Titania falls in love with this absurd figure and can no longer see the real person beneath the illusion. Behind the laughter is a serious question: if someone loves only an image, can that truly be called love?

### 007 — The Misunderstanding Deepens

The magic makes everyone believe they have finally spoken the truth, while their actions keep pushing others farther away. Hermia, Lysander, and those pursuing them hurt one another in the forest, and exhaustion makes every argument sharper. Love does not automatically make them kinder. Instead, it exposes their fear and their need to possess.

### 008 — Undoing the Mistake

Oberon sees that his command has spun out of control, and finally admits that this is not a game he can manipulate without consequence. He orders Puck to remove the mistaken spells one by one, and commands the forest to become still. The first step in correcting a wrong is not to explain that no harm was intended. It is to stop making others bear the consequences.

### 009 — Recognition Before Dawn

Dawn is near, and the magic begins to fade. Four young people wake, one after another, from exhaustion and confusion. They remember the arguments of the night, but cannot tell which feelings truly belong to them. No one speaks first, as if each is waiting for the other to prove that the night was only a dream.

### 010 — Dream and Scar

The lovers support one another as they walk out of the forest. Sunlight passes through the branches, and everything that happened seems like a dream, but that does not make it meaningless. After being misrecognized, pursued, and controlled, they begin to understand that desire without choice and responsibility can easily become harm. To wake is not to forget. It is to carry the memory back into reality.

### 011 — The Duke's Choice

At dawn, Duke Theseus comes hunting in the forest and finds the young people beneath the trees. He could simply enforce the old decree, but he listens to their own voices. He decides that marriage should not be only a command from the family. The city's order remains, but for the first time it makes room for personal choice.

### 012 — Wedding and Stage

Back in Athens, the wedding rearranges three relationships in three different ways. The craftsmen perform their clumsy tragedy. Death on the stage becomes a joke, while the people watching catch a glimpse of their own fears. Theatre does not solve their lives for them. It only gives them a chance to look at their choices from the outside.

### 013 — After the Dream

The wedding ends, the guests depart, and Puck remains alone on the stage, asking the audience to take it all as a dream. But perhaps a dream is simply the part of reality that dares not speak plainly. People desire, make mistakes, and hurt one another, yet they can still learn how to love again. The real magic is waking up willing to take responsibility for the choices we have made.

## English Closing — After Scene 013

And so, like the traveler at the end of an odyssey, we return changed rather than untouched. The forest remains behind us, but its questions travel on: whom do we love, what do we choose, and what must we repair when our choices cause harm? The dream ends, and the journey of becoming responsible for one another begins.
